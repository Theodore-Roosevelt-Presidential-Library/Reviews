# Review Response Playbook

**Status:** Draft for review — Matt Briney, CCMO
**Last updated:** August 12, 2026

This is the policy for how the Library answers public reviews. It exists so that responses
are consistent, fast, and recognisably ours no matter who writes them.

---

## Why we respond at all

Three reasons, in order of how much they matter.

**The reviewer is a guest.** Someone took twenty minutes to tell us about their day. On a
five-star review that deserves acknowledgment; on a one-star review it deserves a fix. This
is hospitality, not reputation management.

**Everyone after them is reading.** The person deciding whether to drive four hours from
Bismarck reads the negative reviews first and reads our replies second. A calm, specific
answer to a complaint does more for that decision than the complaint does against it.

**Google and TripAdvisor both weight owner responsiveness.** Replying is a ranking input on
the two platforms that drive our discovery.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **CCMO (Matt)** | Owns the policy. Personally reviews anything in Tier 1 before it posts. |

No one outside this list posts in the Library's voice.

## Tiers and turnaround

| Tier | What it is | Respond within | Who writes | Who approves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Escalate** | 1–2 stars, or *any* rating alleging safety, discrimination, accessibility failure, staff misconduct, or a factual error about TR or the Library | **24 hours** | Comms | CCMO, before posting |
| **2 — Recover** | 3 stars, or a negative review about a fixable operational problem | **3 days** | Comms | Peer read |
| **3 — Acknowledge** | 4–5 stars that still name something specific to fix | **7 days** | Comms | None |
| **4 — Thank** | Unqualified praise, especially detailed or from a first-time visitor | **14 days**, sampled — not every one | Comms | None |

The dashboard computes these tiers and sorts the queue by how overdue each item is. If
something has been sitting for a week, it will be at the top and it will be red.

**A note on Tier 4.** Do not reply to every five-star review. A wall of identical thank-yous
reads as automated and devalues the replies that matter. Answer the ones with real substance —
someone who drove nine hours, someone whose kid loved the wildlife room — and let the rest
stand on their own.

## Voice

Our brand voice applies here exactly as it does everywhere else: a knowledgeable ranger who's
glad you came, not an institution issuing a statement.

**Do**

- Answer as a person. "We're sorry" and "you're right" are complete, adequate sentences.
- Be specific. "We're adding accessible-parking signage this fall" beats "we value your feedback."
- Concede the point when it's correct. Most operational complaints we've received are true.
- Say what changes, and only if it actually will.
- Keep it to three short paragraphs. Nobody reads a long reply.
- Sign as the Library, not as an individual.

**Don't**

- Don't use "world-class," "transformative," "journey," or "experience" as a noun. Banned in
  the style guide; twice as grating in a reply.
- Don't argue, correct tone, or explain why the reviewer misunderstood — even when they did.
- Don't quote Theodore Roosevelt in a complaint response. Using TR to deflect criticism is
  the single most embarrassing thing we could do.
- Don't paste the same sentence into ten replies. Reviewers read each other's.
- Don't promise a timeline unless Visitor Services has confirmed it.
- Don't offer refunds or comps publicly. Move that to email.
- Don't get defensive about ticket price. State the value; don't litigate it.

## Moving off-platform

Take it to email when the reply would need a refund, a personal apology, an account detail,
or anything about a specific staff member.

> We'd like to make this right and would rather not do it in public. Please email us at
> hello@trlibrary.com and reference this review — we'll pick it up from there.

Post that publicly, then handle it privately. The visible reply matters as much as the
resolution, because it shows every future reader that we engage.

## Templates

Starting points. Every one of these needs editing before it posts — a reply that reads as a
template defeats the purpose.

### Tier 1 — a serious complaint

> Thank you for telling us about this, and we're sorry — this isn't the visit we want anyone
> to have.
>
> [Name the specific thing. State what's being done, concretely.]
>
> We'd like to follow up directly. Please email hello@trlibrary.com and reference this review.
>
> — The team at the Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library

### Tier 2 — a fixable operational problem

> Thank you for the honest feedback. You're right about [the specific thing], and we're
> working on it — [what is actually changing].
>
> We opened in July and we're still learning where the friction is. Notes like this are how
> we find it.
>
> — The team at the Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library

### Tier 3 — happy visitor, specific gripe

> Thank you — glad [the specific thing they loved] landed. And thank you for flagging
> [the gripe]; that one's on our list.
>
> — The team at the Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library

### Tier 4 — substantive praise

> Thank you — this made our day. [React to one specific detail they mentioned.] Come back
> and see us.
>
> — The team at the Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library

## Standing lines on recurring themes

These are the issues our reviews actually raise, as of August 2026. Keep this section current;
it's the part that goes stale fastest. **Verify each claim with Visitor Services before it
goes into a live reply** — several are written as intentions, not confirmed commitments.

| Theme | Line to work from |
|---|---|
| **Walk-up expectations** | Admission is timed-entry and dates sell out well ahead. We're making that clearer earlier — on the site, in listings, and on the approach. |
| **Accessible parking** | Accessible parking and drop-off exist but are genuinely hard to find. Signage is a priority fix. |
| **Wayfinding** | Additional signage from the highway through to the entrance is in progress. |
| **Parking capacity** | Peak-season demand has outrun the lot. We're looking at overflow and a shuttle. |
| **Crowding and bottlenecks** | Summer volume exceeded projections. We're adjusting how timed-entry slots are released. |
| **Museum store pricing** | Acknowledge it, don't defend it. Note that it's been shared with the store team. |
| **Water availability** | Fair point in the Badlands. Additional water stations are being added. |
| **Interpretation of TR's record** | Engage seriously, never defensively. The exhibits present a full life including its contradictions, and we welcome the argument. Do not litigate specific historical claims in a review reply — if a reviewer raises a factual dispute, escalate to Tier 1 and verify before responding. |

**On that last row.** We have already received a thoughtful four-star review arguing the
exhibits soften TR's record on race and empire. That critique will recur, it will eventually
come from a historian or a journalist, and a defensive reply would become the story. The
answer is to take it seriously in public and to route anything factual through the CCMO and
the scholarly team before we say a word.

## How the dashboard knows you replied

It re-reads the review. Google, TripAdvisor and Yelp all return the owner's response
alongside the review text, so posting a reply on the platform is enough — there is no
box to tick here, and no second system to keep in sync.

The queue clears on the next collection: within a day for a recent review, within a week
for an older one, because reply detection on older reviews rides on the weekly deep
refresh. If you reply to something from a month ago and it is still showing in the queue
tomorrow, that is expected — not a bug.

Two consequences worth knowing. A reply posted and then deleted will show as answered
until the next deep refresh. And a reply left from a personal account rather than the
Page or Business Profile will not be detected at all, because the platform does not
attach it to the listing as an owner response.

## Getting to a review from the queue

Each item in the queue has two links, and they go to different places.

**Open this review** is a deep link to that single review on the platform. Use it to read the
review in context and see anything the queue truncated. Google's version opens that one review
on its own — it is not the listing.

**Reply on Google / TripAdvisor / Yelp** opens the owner tools, which is where a reply is
actually posted. The platforms do not offer a link that opens a specific review *with the reply
box already on it*, so this is one step short of that: open the owner tools, then find the
review by the reviewer's name and date.

**Yelp is the exception.** The scraper returns no per-review link, so those items show
**Find on Yelp**, which opens the listing. As of the next collection run the collector will
build a Yelp permalink whenever the actor supplies a review id, and the button will change on
its own. There are currently five Yelp reviews in total, so this is small.

## Reviewing this policy

Revisit quarterly, and immediately if any of these happen:

- A single theme appears in more than 20% of reviews in a 30-day window
- The 30-day average drops more than 0.3 against the prior period
- Any review triggers press interest
- Response rate falls below 80% of the triage queue

The dashboard surfaces the first two automatically.

## The AI question

This needs its own section because it is the most polarising thing in the building.

The wristband, the AI-generated period portraits, and the AI Roosevelt are the single most
praised feature in our reviews — and the single most common reason someone gives us one or
two stars. A small, articulate minority finds it cheapening. The phrase "AI slop" appears in
our reviews more than once. A related objection is about **data**: visitors are asked for a
name, an email, and a photograph at entry, and some are uncomfortable with that.

**How to respond**

- Do not defend the technology or explain how it works. They understood it; they disliked it.
- Say plainly that the AI stations are optional, that the original TR objects, films, and
  traditional displays stand on their own, and that a visitor can skip registration entirely
  and still see everything. This is true and it is the single most useful thing we can say.
- On the data question, state what actually happens to a visitor's photo and email, and how
  long it is kept. **Confirm the current retention policy before answering — do not guess.**
- Never say "most visitors love it." It reads as dismissal.
- Do not treat this as a tier-4 nuisance. It is the most likely subject of a critical
  feature story about the Library, and our replies are the public record of how seriously
  we took it.

There is already a good reply on file — a July response to a three-star review that led with
the stations being optional. That's the model. Reuse its logic, not its wording.

## Where we're starting from

From the full dataset as of August 12, 2026: **335 reviews, 4.78 blended, one of which has a
reply.** Sixty-four are in the response queue and fifty-nine of those are past the SLAs above.

The critical set clusters into exactly three stories, which is unusually tractable:

1. **Timed entry and walk-ups** — visitors driving long distances, arriving without tickets,
   and being turned away or facing multi-hour waits. This produces our worst reviews and it
   is a messaging problem more than an operations one.
2. **The AI experience** — see the section above.
3. **Crowding** — the only theme whose share is rising.

Twenty reviews predate the July 4 public opening, averaging 4.4. Most are press, donor, and
Founding Member preview visits and are entirely legitimate. Three low-rated ones from July
1–2 are worth a look, but do not assume bad faith — check whether they attended a preview
before treating them as anything else.

The first task under this policy is clearing the backlog, starting with the eight critical
items that have been waiting between six and thirty-two days.
